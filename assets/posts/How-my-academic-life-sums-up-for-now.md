When I was young, even as a child, I think I always had strong ambitions. At a very early age I wanted to become some CEO of a company, or if I couldn't write that in kindergarten homework, I'd just draw an astronaut because that's what everyone else would draw or write, but I always had plans and ambitions for myself.

I had a good clear goal of my future early on. There was this little project we did in sixth grade, a sort of roadmap, my teacher at the time called it the Vision Board, what your future looked like. I studied in Angono Christian School Inc, and once I graduated elementary, I would take up high school in Rizal National Science High School and finally cap it off with the University of the Philippines. Very ambitious roadmap, I know, but that's pretty much how it was going.

RISCI was really prestigious and really hard to get into. For almost all of grade six I was preparing, staying in my room answering practice books for the entrance exam while other kids my age were outside playing. I didn't have any backup plan, only the one. The goal was that I would only go into Rizal National Science High School, and that meant I would only go there. After a lot of screenings with no backup, I got in, and I really did what I could to enjoy learning.

I particularly enjoyed robotics and went on to participate, and lose, which I guess is partly my fault, but I always enjoyed participating and appreciated the people who even at that early age saw something I would become, or maybe some fraction of what I could be. They were really nice to me, but I was a really troublesome kid, stingy attitude too, I'm so sorry to everyone I gave attitude to, but until now I still have an attitude, oh.

Among those competitions, I remember Dr. Jossette Biyo giving some inspirational words at an event closing after we lost. I know we lost, it was sad losing by some technicalities, but during that speech I was really inspired and thankful for what DOST had done for me personally at the time, which wasn't really anything they did to me directly at all, but it inspired me, and the people who ran the event were really cool and motivating. So on top of the ambitions I already had, I wanted to work at DOST after I graduated college. That was the plan.

The pandemic came, kind of sucked in the middle of high school, and it really just stopped everything. Most of the remainder of high school going into college prep is pretty much a blur. As we neared graduation and the practices leading up to it, I was devastated. I got covid and couldn't attend my own graduation. I was destroyed, had to watch a livestream instead, just looking at my own face on a screen where the ceremony was happening without me there. What would have been a way to really close out that chapter of my life just left me in a confused state. All my high school life, all that practice, kind of just led into nothing.

Around the same time, we were preparing which colleges to go into, and I of course only had one thing in mind. My mind was dead set on UP, and I was going on a review center getting as proficient as someone could during the pandemic. But it was the same thing all over again. Covid got in the way, UPCAT got cancelled, admissions went by grades alone, which for me weren't enough. I was devastated, destroyed, and I cried. My vision roadmap was shattered.

I wouldn't have had a college at all since I was dead set on UP, but family told me I should always have a backup, contrary to before where I was just dead set on one thing. My backup was PUP, and I was fortunate enough to get into my first choice of course, BSECE.

First year, studying as someone who studies in the pandemic, there's only so much you can do when the odds are against you. I was good where I was good and bad in calculus. We did have calculus in high school, but I was excused from those classes for competitions, missed a lot of the early fundamentals, and after losing in those competitions I'd go back not understanding calculus because they were already done with the fundamentals, and I was just completely fucked. That reflected in my first semester of college, the defining stain on my record, a 2.75 in Calculus 1, effectively disqualifying me from Latin honors. I tried to ignore my emotions at the time, but realizing now the grades I could've gotten, it's a bit of a sting, unfortunately really stupid in calculus that year, and as much as I tried I just didn't have a decent professor to help with it.

Calculus 1 was a bust, but Calculus 2 had a really strict prof who really pushed my nuts in, and I'd arguably say a good way, some people didn't see it that way but I did, because I got an improved score of 2.25. Shit, but something I was really really proud of, and I still am. I did my best to redeem myself from Calculus 1, but I'd already been disqualified from Latin honors. Any good grade from here on out was just me learning because I enjoyed learning. Vying for Latin honors does add some pressure, and in hindsight seeing what could've been, it still stings that I could've qualified somewhat, but anyways, for the rest of college the focus was just to learn and graduate.

For a bit of history on that later part, I was able to create a really small but gray business. If a business isn't registered, some might call it illegal, and this was illegal in the sense that it wasn't registered, and in the sense that it was a gray area market.

It started off as just a hobby thing I did in second year of college when I had time to kill. Basically it was computer vision and machine learning to create an assistive tool of sorts, which I eventually was told to call such, "tool."

To my surprise people bought when I would just sell them by handing them the files. For a while I was surprised by how that worked. That was my first introduction to SaaS.

Soon I got busy, but not too busy, so I decided I needed a way to automate selling. Back then Sellix was a thing, where people of my kind would also sell their "tools." So I got a Sellix site up, and I connected the webhook to Discord. In Discord I had a Discord bot which I had hosted on Pterodactyl for very cheap, 300 PHP covered a year, and for a while I had my own Sellix website that people could go to and buy my "tool." It was cool to see it work in the background. Occasionally people would open a support ticket here and there, but for the most part I was happy to get that working. It was indeed very cool.

For a while it was working good while I was busy, until it wasn't. I became a victim of the FBI and Interpol as collateral damage from Operation Talent. Sellix got shut down and I hadn't had the time to restart that e-commerce side to really keep it going, and the remaining users were dwindling so it wasn't making sense to really pay for the infra cost. It would still get visitors every week, up to now it still gets visitors and traction, but without a website and without the systems I had painstakingly built, they kind of just got yanked from me. At the time I was busy with thesis, which was really taking me by a chokehold, so I had little to no time nor the mental capacity for what would be some ideas to bring back the site or the webhook integrations.

Having such a shaft up my ass is indeed something, but whether I liked it or not may be the interesting part of this story, because that shaft was quantum image processing.

I knew going into thesis that I wanted to continue doing something with image processing because I loved it and I was proficient in it, hobby learning, tangible somethings out of it, machine learning, computer vision, morphological transforms, all on a good basis. And AI was kind of shit at the time too, which was why I didn't get too much into AI use. I always felt like I could do better than AI, which is why I did better than AI.

I had this bizarre title going into thesis for the first time. Other people were going with their own silly electronics titles, which my adviser called trash. Mine was unique because it was my fixations, computer vision and, weirdly, mimosa pudicas. When my adviser saw it, it set a precedent. He called me an alien, because he hadn't seen anything like it. It was rejected, simply because it was weird, and I think he was confused in a perplexed good way. The main thing, I think, was that it didn't fit engineering. That's why it got rejected. He didn't necessarily reject it because it was bad.

A couple of classmates were discussing what fields were likely for my adviser to accept: AI/ML but not YOLOv8 because too many people were proposing it, 5G, VR, blockchain, FPGA, drones, and quantum. AI and ML was my bread and butter, but I got put down when I heard the adviser didn't like YOLOv8, too many people, and also nobody could get it down to a tee. I would've been really confident with it, but ah, I guess not, I guess I'd be stuck with quantum. Shiny new object syndrome, a bit of that too. No one in ECE had done quantum, and some classmates said hey, I should go for it because they thought I could. I also didn't have much to lose by then anyway, not with Latin honors already off the table and the 2.75 already sitting on my record, so there wasn't much pressure left holding me back from just taking the risk. So I thought why not.

I created three new title ideas to present to my prof. My talking was really fucking shit, but I showed quantum image processing for morphological transforms, quantum image processing for feature detection, KAZE or AKAZE since SIFT had a license so that was a no go, and something about quantum moments or quantum kernels. Keep in mind, at this time I knew jack shit about quantum. Basically my idea was quantum makes things fast, and if I slap that onto something I knew was slow, that was how I justified the problem and the solution. Big fucking mistake. But it was enough to impress the professor. He personally vouched for me and I was set with an internship with DOST ASTI, since he'd also be our OJT adviser. And this tied right back into the DOST dream I'd carried since that speech in RISCI, working there had been the plan since Dr. Biyo's closing remarks all those years back, so getting to actually go was amazing, and I was so, so, so excited.

At the time AI was getting better and I would converse with it for ideas. Another big mistake. AI was getting capable, but it did not mean it was right.

Now this is where it exploded. I got my answers and information from AI. I said before that I could do better than AI when learning, but this time I had no gauge for myself to know if I was doing better than it. So inevitably, I used it, and the minor hallucinations started to show in the things I learned. These frontier providers claimed research capability, but it wasn't really capable of research at the time. These AIs drew conclusions, twisted nuance, and for a long time led me astray.

But the technical stuff wasn't really what was brutal to me. It was the workflow of learning from AI that was causing the cognitive debt. Back when I was at the helm of designing things myself, I knew everything, every system, like the back of my hand. Here, that wasn't the case. When presenting and asked why, I'd think, I don't know why. My talking, where did it go. I usually have an answer to this stuff. I didn't notice it at first, but in hindsight this was where it was starting to eat me. When you use AI, you don't do the things necessary to get to the conclusions you form, and there's no nuance on the AI's part, no evaluation of relevance, difference, or nuance that truly determines what gets made of research. A human with logic understands the difference. AI, no. That's cognitive debt.

Our professor told us we should use AI or we'd be left out, and that was true, curating your own RRL would take too long, you'd miss too much, you couldn't hit the fifty or so recommended papers, absurd for undergrad now that I think about it, that's dissertation-level. So on one hand we needed to use it. But when we presented, we gave the professor an AI ick, and he told us we shouldn't let AI do the thinking for us. Really confusing. Use it, but get punished for using it, what is it. One thing was clear: no one really knew how to use AI. We were all stupid little shits with fucking machine guns. Someone always starts with a pistol, but because AI was getting progressively better and the adoption was scaring people, it was a no brainer someone should use it anyway.

This would continue week after week. Brutal comes to brutal, it was brutal indeed. The professor got progressively meaner, the class got progressively scared, anxiety swelling, loss of motivation for the lost little shits we were. No one understood how to use AI, no one could think at the time. But out of all of that, there was me, with the remainder of my critical thinking left, aware enough to see my own state was miserable. I reflected, and the only way out of the hole was to keep showing up, piece together the critical feedback under a layer of screams and mean remarks. I knew it was there, I just needed to filter the mean stuff out. I'm glad I was able to reflect and realize it, not a lot were able to see it, I think, only me. Every Saturday he'd wait for someone to present and nobody would, asses glued to their chairs, he'd scream at them and still nobody would go. I always took the initiative. I would present. There would be times I'd be yelled at on the first slide and told to sit, but I'd weed out the screaming, take my shivering best shot at pulling out the bad parts, apply it, and get to the next slide the following week. It was fucking slow. But there was a pace.

But alas, that pace meant nothing. I came to regressing due to the progressive rude comments. Until there came something.

My prof was looking for programmers, I got suggested, we met in his office to discuss a job, this would be Pailon, an empty offer, I accepted anyway, same as I'd taken a job the semester prior for another prof's master thesis. At the end of that meeting I asked him if quantum was really worth it, if I could just do AI and computer vision instead. He said, but you're already good at that, if I did that I'd only be doing a project, I wouldn't be using intelligence. It wouldn't be research, what I was doing with quantum was research. If I already knew what I was doing, it wouldn't be research. Sayang talino mo.

Wow. That was something. I went cold, thought there was an earthquake, asked, there wasn't, they said I was stressed, no sleep, which was true, but what he said painted everything. I was leagues ahead of everyone, doing something even my prof didn't know, why I was called an alien, why he was mean, why everything, it clicked, and I knew he saw something in me, and at that time the misery and anxiety kind of faded. The anxiety didn't fully fade, my heart truly pounded whenever he'd chat me, but boy, what words have blessed and grazed my ears.

Third year ended with no defense date, so it was OJT time. I went ham, studied and studied the right way, eventually learned how stupid I was for slapping quantum onto everything thinking it'd make things faster, no, that's not how quantum works. One thing that did kinda work was quantum image representations, so I studied the shit out of that. I ditched AI for a while, one because using it on DOST ASTI premises was embarrassing even though they used it too, I just didn't like using it in public. We were recommended to actually learn quantum properly, quantum mechanics, IBM's quantum courses, and being okayish at Python and a slightly better than average skid who knows how to read documentation, I got better, progressively better, and being able to just learn again like that felt so, so good. It showed me that having a learning workflow was the chef's kiss to everything.

At the end of my time with ASTI, I thanked everyone there for having me and taking care of me, told them working there had been my dream. They just smiled, thought it was cute, I know there might be some layers to why they found it cute or amusing, that's not really for me to understand. As far as I'm concerned, I was happy, and if it was cute or amusing, it didn't really matter. I was happy.

Soon the title defense schedule got announced, mid-OJT, so we went back to the Saturday sessions. With the knowledge from DOST ASTI I formed a new proposal, QARQI, and with the slow-paced learning from before applied, I presented like I knew I could, and I was able to defend everything. My adviser sat through the whole thing, listening eagerly, never saw him like that. He held a satisfied face, a grin, said I really was an alien, and I was approved for title defense. Title defense itself was bad, second to worst placing in the panel's scores, but it didn't matter, I had my professor's support. He was still rude, hadn't learned how to be nice yet, but this sealed the deal. My prof saw something in me, and now I saw it too.

The panel gave significant feedback for why QARQI couldn't continue. Out of the remainder of leftover ideas from ASTI, I created MHRQI instead.

This was now an internal struggle. I was in between worlds, in ASTI I could take my time, less mean and rude comments, none even, but here I still needed a filter of sorts, though by now I had my grounding. Every day I'd wonder how I'd use AI, look for tools to help me learn with AI present, and there just wasn't any such tool. That left me really confused. So I fixed things myself. I hated how AI would write, so I fixed that first, went into a rabbit hole of grammar knowledge. Then I went into AI analysis, learned to constrain it with instructions so it wouldn't assume everything, and it kinda worked. I was able to tweak the AI's behavior into something that would assist me and accelerate my tasks while I stayed at the helm, still understanding what I needed to understand, I guess, I did.

There was one way to know for sure. Whenever I slept I had dreams and epiphanies, I'd go lucid and solve the problems of my thesis in my dreams, since I needed the time, during the day I thought, during sleep in my dreams I'd also think. I needed to think all the time because I was making something novel, nobody had done it, I needed to think from scratch, itch my technical creativity, and I'm proud of that creativity, contributing algorithms to a growing infant literature instead of piecing together what already existed.

In the presentations, my classmates were truly scared beyond words, and slowly I was improving, really improving, going on tangents but progressively getting less all over the place. It felt good, because part of what makes AI good is it can go on with creativity, matching my own, and when it doesn't lead you astray by structuring the workflow so hallucination doesn't happen, it seemed like it could indeed work. I was plainly surprised.

With cognitive debt adequately solved, and my talking helped along by actually-progressively less rude comments from my prof, it was looking up. New goal: present cohesively. Took three sessions or less, and I got it.

Final defense was coming, and everyone needed to present to him individually first, results and the whole thing finished, about six months of experimentation with MHRQI. I made two algorithms in it, one quantum image representation, one a bit shitty quantum denoising algorithm. The representation algorithm I polished out really well. The denoising method, let's say you could tell it was homemade, quantum denoising from Temu, mom can we have quantum denoising at home, we have quantum denoising at home. It worked though. Not as impressive as the representation work, that was what took the cake, but it sufficed for what it needed to be, and that was enough.

I presented my findings honestly, admitted the denoising algorithm was pretty lackluster in some areas, but said something I think was right in that moment, that I found it interesting, the behavior I noticed while trying and failing and trying and failing until I discovered this, something related to Grover's algorithm and the diffusion part, and that even if the algorithms didn't win across the charts, the important thing was that this was research, honest, unaltered, not doctored, virgin results, elegantly put into how I interpreted the observation and its implications. My professor listened eagerly like before, asked each component one by one as if learning it himself, this and this uses this, and I explained, yes or no, and could talk. Cognitive debt was just not there. I knew the implications, the reasons, the surprising discoveries, and when I shared that he was satisfied. I was told I could go forth with the defense, first to be confirmed, the only one who ever went through with the one-on-one presentation he required, since no one else did, too scared. I was happy. My prof said I was the only one who passes in his eyes.

Final defense came, and it was on the day of my birthday of all days. I came confident and happy, wanted to be the best defense so bad. A junior of mine was watching too. I defended like I practiced, nailed it in fourteen minutes. The problem was the panel didn't understand it. They said they read the paper and didn't understand it, listened to the presentation and did not understand it. They asked questions and I perfectly debated my answers, but they did not understand it. They asked about the results, I answered as honestly as I could, and my junior's account was there was no better way of answering than I did. For a moment the panel was happy, despite the unimpressive results, impressed I was honest, that I valued the true meaning of research. In the end they said they couldn't fault me for any technical stuff, that what I did surpassed all they knew, so to their eyes, since they didn't really know, my technical stuff must be good.

To my dismay, I got a barely excellent score. It destroyed me, building all that confidence and prowess for people who admitted they weren't on my level, only for them to fail to give me the score I deserved. Everyone in my class knew I deserved it. You know who got high marks? Those who weren't allowed by my professor to go onto the defense, and they scored higher than me. I don't know how, and I don't need to talk about them or what happened, it was the least of what I expected from my birthday. My junior told me my work was history to PUP. But it wasn't appreciated, it wasn't all I ever got up and hoped to be. Like I was Tai Lung and my prof was Shifu, who would I be if I wasn't the best that day, I didn't know, I couldn't know, everything was upside down that day, on my birthday too. Not easy to stomach at all. All that work, all that mental sacrifice, all that chasing and exceeding everyone's expectations far beyond what they knew about me, only to have exceeded the comprehension of normietards, something I had no words to describe, and I still don't know how to explain it now. It is brutal.

A lot of saltiness after that, and I mean a lot, and it will never fully end, I'll forever be salty, but life is life and we needed to continue. Thesis was done and defended successfully, but I was chasing excellence, not just a pass. My prof messaged, said it was okay, he understood, said we'd get it published instead so someone would appreciate the work. Both QARQI and MHRQI went to journals, I picked journal over conference since I didn't want to talk in front of a lot of people, still waiting on publication to this day, hasn't been too long, happy enough it'll go somewhere, be immortalized somehow, calms me for the time being, maybe not enough, but I think it suffices, I don't have as much bloodlust anymore, exaggeration, but you get the point.

Same prof asked what we'd do after college, whether we'd work, go into academe, or go into research, and they didn't know, but I did. I said business, since I'd had a small shitty business before and liked building it, and I also wanted to try DOST ASTI again for the passion I'd newly found for quantum. When he asked why, I said AI solutions around education, because of the realizations I'd had working on Pailon and research suggesting AI was making our generation more stupid, and that was the catalyst. My personal reason went back to how I'd felt before, since nobody should ever have to suffer cognitive debt with AI the way I did, and I wanted people to accelerate with AI instead of being hollowed out by it, through a product that actually considered the state of mind of the user and gave them a real workflow the same way I cleared my own path. I knew I had a lot to contribute since I'd actually made something that worked. If you hadn't been reading, I have some evidence above.

My prof shared the sentiment and wanted in, since he's an educator who values education and impact, and that's what I want too. After months of conceptualizing, he told me to do it right and do it with people, and to make it legal this time so it could actually reach the students and institutions it was meant for, and that's the vision of all of it.

If I hadn't done it legally, I don't know what it would've been. But now that I am, in hindsight I've been able to land onto the path I've always dreamed of.

That's my current origin of DADO, comprised of me and some people. The vision and mission is built on my own experience and my own suffering.

I had always wanted to be a CEO, even in kindergarten, and now I'm a sole proprietor of my own business. I'm not pretending that DADO is anything at all yet, or that it's done anything to earn the name of a business. But I’ve made it my mission to go about proceeding with that ambition and make it into reality.

And I think there's still a future left in that Vision Board I made back in sixth grade. UP was never actually lost, I think I just hadn't gotten to it yet. I'm planning on taking a master's in AI next, at UP itself, so I can do a good job with DADO, and if I do that, I'd get to exceed the original goal, which was only ever to graduate there. On top of that I've got a new ambition now too, a really stupid one, to become a laureate someday.
